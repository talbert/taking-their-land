Historical report: superseded

This is the 2026-08-16 exploratory report on 407 events, preserved as a historical checkpoint. Its claims of "eight axes and six stable kinds" and sorting "by the data rather than by theory" were superseded by later review. The original results and wording below are retained for the record, not presented as current conclusions.

Later review found no tested automatic partition robust enough to serve as the public taxonomy. The current project distinguishes six source-reviewed editorial families, eight-trait analytical profiles, and nine canonical v6 exploratory factors. Taking their land · empirical re-categorization

Six Shapes of Taking

407 historical events, scored on 60 measured dimensions, sorted by the data rather than by theory. The result is eight axes and six stable kinds — and they do not agree with the atlas's current twelve categories.

Exploratory factor analysis · oblimin rotation · bootstrap-validated k-means · 2026-08-16

The finding

Mechanism and severity are independent

The single most useful result is a negative one. Lethal coercion emerged as its own factor — the fifth largest — loading on lethality, mortality share, subsistence destruction, confinement, and sex-differential targeting, and on almost nothing else. It is statistically separable from how a process was organized, whether settlers arrived, and whether land changed hands in law.

That is the atlas's own editorial rule, recovered from data that never saw it: categories describe mechanisms, not moral severity. Violence is a real, measurable property of these events, but it varies across structural types rather than defining them. A taxonomy built on structure will not smuggle in a severity ranking, and one built on severity would cut across every structural boundary in the corpus.

The corroboration is direct: comparing the 222 events currently on the atlas against the 185 that are not, the difference in mean lethality is +0.03 — indistinguishable from zero. Whatever rule has governed inclusion, it has not been a violence filter.

Plate II · the six kinds

Six stable shapes

Clusters were chosen on bootstrap stability, not on silhouette. Silhouette peaked at k=10, but only 4 of those 10 clusters survived resampling. At k=6, all six survive (mean Jaccard 0.842) — the highest stability of any solution tested. Bars show each cluster's mean position on the eight axes; the centre line is the corpus average.

Is it real structure?

Overlapping kinds, not sharp boxes

Absolute silhouette values are low — around 0.23 — which on its own would suggest a formless continuum. That reading is wrong, and the test that settles it is a comparison against a matched-covariance unimodal null: data with the same means, variances, and correlations but no cluster structure at all.

Real data beats that null by 26 to 32 standard deviations. The structure is emphatically present. What the low silhouette actually says is that the six kinds have populated boundaries — a great many genuine hybrids sit between the cores rather than in them.

The practical consequence: the six shapes are best used as named regions of a continuous eight-dimensional space, not as boxes every case must enter. An event's coordinates carry more information than its cluster label, and for boundary cases the label alone is actively misleading. A revised atlas should record the coordinates and treat the name as a convenience.

Old scheme vs. new

Which current categories survive

Agreement between the six emergent kinds and the current twelve mechanism families is ARI 0.29 — real but partial overlap. Reading family by family shows the pattern is not uniform: some current labels are coherent structural kinds, and others are names applied to processes that behave nothing alike.

Reading the fragmentation

Two labels are doing no work

State resettlement / internal colonization scatters across five of the six kinds, with only 33% in its largest. Expulsion / deportation / ethnic cleansing does nearly the same — five kinds, 35% largest. These are not mechanisms in any structural sense. They are descriptions of intent or of legal character that get applied to processes with fundamentally different architectures: some install a replacement population under formal title, some empty a region and leave it empty, some move people into space that is already occupied.

The contrast is sharp. Population exchange / partition lands entirely in one kind. Conflict displacement is 97% concentrated, and demographic expansion analogue 96%. Those three are earning their keep as categories — they pick out events that genuinely resemble each other.

Note what this implies about the prehistoric cases. The “expansion analogue” label has always carried a faint suggestion that these are lesser or merely inferred examples of the real thing. The data say the opposite: they form one of the tightest, most internally consistent groups in the entire corpus. Their coherence is a finding, and the word analogue understates it.

Coverage

What the atlas selects for

Comparing the 222 rendered cases against the 185 that are not, the current inclusion rule becomes legible as a position in factor space. The atlas selects strongly for settler implantation, legal-proprietary machinery, and durable outcomes — and selects against state orchestration and displacement into already-occupied space.

This is a coherent editorial definition of settler colonialism, and defensible as such. But it leaves two large, internally consistent regions of the space nearly unrepresented: transient conflict displacement (4 of 66 rendered) and state demographic engineering (17 of 61). Whether that is the right boundary is an editorial question, not a statistical one — but it is now a visible and quantified choice rather than an implicit one.

Re-mapping

Where the model disagrees with the atlas

Every one of the 407 events carries cluster and coordinates in final_k6_assignments.csv. Three groups are worth direct attention.

Off-atlas events sitting in atlas-dense kinds

These resemble what the atlas already publishes, but are not on it. The prehistoric turnovers among them were found by this project's gap scan and are absent from the audit register entirely.

Rendered cases in sparsely-covered kinds

Thirty-two published cases sit in kinds the atlas otherwise mostly excludes — chiefly the MENA Jewish departures and the Soviet national deportations. They are not errors; they are the places where the atlas has already, quietly, crossed its own boundary. That is worth making explicit rather than leaving as an unexamined exception.

Genuine hybrids

Lowest-silhouette rendered cases — those sitting almost equidistant between kinds. Any single label misrepresents them, and they are the strongest argument for recording coordinates over categories.

Limits

What would change these numbers

One factor is era-confounded. Durability of outcome correlates with start year at ρ = −0.51 — older events score as more durable, partly because they have had millennia to demonstrate it. Treat F6 as contaminated by observation window; the other seven are below |0.45| and lethality is clean at ρ = 0.00.

  • Cross-coder agreement is 0.76, not 0.93. The pilot's 0.93 was two instances of one model; the honest figure comes from two different models coding the same 30 events — 0.76 exact, 0.95 within one anchor step. Measurement noise attenuates correlations, so true factor structure is likely somewhat sharper than measured.
  • N=407 against 60 dimensions is thin for factor analysis. Robustness runs help: excluding the 33 gap-scan events reproduces loadings at congruence 0.96–1.00. But restricting to the 222 on-atlas cases collapses F8 entirely (congruence 0.06) — coerced exogenous labor is a real axis that the atlas's current selection cannot see.
  • Coding is model-generated, anchored and validated but not an expert panel. It is a consistent instrument, not a substitute for domain scholarship.
  • The corpus inherits the register's selection. The gap scan mitigates this — it found 33 qualifying absences — but cannot eliminate it.
Recommendations

What to do with this

  • Retire two labels. “State resettlement / internal colonization” and “expulsion / deportation / ethnic cleansing” fragment across five kinds each and describe intent rather than structure. Replace them with the structural distinction the data draw: whether a replacement population arrives, and whether the destination was already occupied.
  • Keep three. Population exchange/partition, conflict displacement, and demographic expansion are structurally coherent. Consider renaming the last to drop “analogue” — it is among the tightest groups in the corpus.
  • Record coordinates, not just a category. Store the eight factor scores per case. For hybrids they are the honest answer, and they let the atlas express degree without inventing more boxes.
  • Decide the boundary deliberately. Conflict displacement and state demographic engineering are near-absent by an implicit rule. Either adopt the exclusion openly in the methods text, or admit the strongest candidates.
  • Review the 33 gap-scan finds on their own merits — independent of this analysis. Soviet dekulakization, the Porfirian Yaqui deportations, and the Argentine Chaco conquest are absences the register's own logic implies should be filled.
