Four new notes on rights and home loss

Occurred October 16, 2026

Four new research notes cover Sonpur, eastern Wisconsin, Norris Farms and Etowah. They add records of reported loss of rights, homes or village use.

Dalton reports that Singrai Manki lost rights and control over 12 villages in Sonpur. This does not establish that all residents left. The date of the loss remains unknown.

Dye reports that Oneota groups drove local groups out or absorbed them in eastern Wisconsin. The cause of the Aztalan departure remains uncertain.

At Norris Farms, Dye reports attacks, flight and a later return. He does not establish permanent loss of the village.

Dye reports that people left Etowah. A severe attack is one possible cause. The Crow Creek note keeps the fire link and later village use uncertain.

These four notes and the Crow Creek correction add no atlas case or model score. The 80 archive records remain inactive.

Slave transport, contract work and island residence

Occurred October 6, 2026

The atlas adds a case on slave transport, contract work and residence in the Mascarenes and Seychelles. It spans 1638 to about 1920. The sources describe selected groups and periods. They do not show that every arrival was forced.

Two links show selected voyages in 1770 and 1807. The points are approximate place references. The arcs are not ship tracks. The departure port for the 1718 arrival in Bourbon is not mapped. Other routes and later fates remain unknown.

The atlas has 540 cases and 552 stops. The active statistical roster still has 676 events. Eleven separate scientific entries remain inactive. The site keeps all 22 earlier update posts.

New atlas case

Kazakh flight and land loss, 1929–1934

Occurred October 6, 2026

The atlas adds a case on flight, forced returns and land loss in Kazakhstan from 1929 to 1934. State plans sought settled life, but many plans failed. The account keeps those plans separate from forced returns, local expulsions and pasture loss.

Pianciola argues that famine caused dependence and some settlement. He rejects violent settlement as the usual cause of the famine. He estimates that one-third of Kazakhs died in 1930–1933. The drop in the republic's Kazakh population also includes migration. These measures are separate. The sources do not give one total for people forced to move.

All eight trait values remain from the fixed model. Each has a source qualification. Filled cells show coding completion, not source confidence. The active statistical roster still has 676 events. The atlas has 539 cases and 551 stops.

  • Niccolò Pianciola, Famine in the steppe (2004), pp. 137–138, 147, 154–156, 163–165, 170–181 and 186. Failed plans, flight, forced return, local expulsion, pasture loss and continued returns.
  • Niccolò Pianciola, Ukraine and Kazakhstan: Comparing the Famines (2018), pp. 441–442. Famine and partial settlement; attributed one-third mortality, separate death-plus-migration decline.

Next search plan

Occurred October 4, 2026
