## Visual and editorial rulings

1. Every new event receives one consolidated playback stop. Source-internal
   phases may be described within the stop, but they are not duplicated as
   adjacent phase cards.
2. Amenhotep II Year 9 shows forced movement from a broad Retjenu/Aphek
   campaign region into Egyptian custody. Memphis is a context marker for the
   king's recorded return, not a claimed final destination for every captive.
3. Muršili II shows a broad forced-removal route from Arzawa into Hatti and a
   simultaneous political-incorporation outcome. The royal aggregate of about
   65,000–66,000 is not added to interim captures or returned Hittite subjects.
4. Nipur-Pasate, Tumme-Habhu, and Zamua show an Assyrian operational approach
   to the affected source region. They do not draw captives to Assyria because
   the source does not identify a captive destination.
5. Upper Tigris/Nirbu keeps its different cohorts separate: prisoners of
   unknown destination, Assyrian famine returnees placed at Tushha, local
   returnees to their own towns, hostages, and later fugitives. Schematic
   internal routes are labeled as such and do not become a single stream.
6. Royal military figures remain attributed claims rather than civilian
   censuses. Unknown resident denominators, return, mortality, descendants,
   and labor assignments remain unknown.
7. Approximate ancient place anchors orient the reader; labels explicitly say
   when a point is a broad or schematic regional anchor rather than an exact
   excavated location.
